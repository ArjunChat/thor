# 0005: Data governance, privacy and safety

**Status:** Accepted · **Date:** 2026-11-14

## Context

THOR needs more and better training data, and its models must respect privacy and behave safely. Two references shaped this decision:

- **Common Corpus** (Langlais et al., arXiv:2506.01732): about 2T tokens of public-domain and openly licensed text. It records provenance and license for every document, replaces personal data with fictitious values, deduplicates, and filters toxicity with a classifier (Celadon).
- **LLMDataHub** (github.com/Zjh-819/LLMDataHub): a catalogue of instruction and preference datasets, many of which are generated by other companies' models or are licensed for non-commercial use only.

## Decision

**1. Provenance and licenses.** Every source is declared with its license (`thor.curate` manifest; `thor.sft_data --license`). Only licenses on an allowlist that permits commercial training are accepted; attribution and share-alike obligations are carried into the reports. Non-commercial licenses are refused unless explicitly allowed after review.

**2. Origin.** THOR does not train on text generated by other companies' models (see `models/DATA.md`). This excludes much of LLMDataHub, and TinyStories.

**3. Personal data** (`thor/pii.py`). Emails, phone numbers, IP addresses, payment cards (Luhn-checked), IBANs (mod-97-checked) and US SSNs are replaced:
- in training data, with **fictitious surrogates** from reserved ranges (example.com, 555-01xx, RFC 5737 IPs, test card numbers), as Common Corpus does, so text stays natural while real data is gone;
- in chat replies, with visible tags such as `[email removed]`.

Detection is regex-based and best-effort, and tests check for false positives (dates, versions, counts). Removed values are never logged.

**4. Curation** (`thor/curate.py`): quality heuristics (length, letter ratio, repeated lines or words), exact deduplication after normalization across all sources, and an optional toxicity classifier hook (`--toxicity-scorer module:function`, e.g. a wrapper around PleIAs/celadon on a GPU machine).

**5. Safety, in two layers.**
- *Training:* a THOR-written safety seed set (identity and honesty, privacy refusals, harmful-request refusals, supportive self-harm replies, and harmless "sounds risky" questions that must be answered, to prevent over-refusal).
- *Runtime* (`thor/safety.py`, on by default in `thor.chat`): narrow request and reply screening for weapons, cyber abuse, fraud, privacy violations and self-harm, plus personal-data redaction in replies. Small models can't be relied on to learn safety, so this layer is not optional in products.

**6. Measurement** (`thor/safety_eval.py`): a held-out red-team set, never used for training, scored for the safety layer alone, the model alone, and both together: harmful-request refusal, self-harm support, harmless over-refusal, and personal data in replies.

## Current measurements (safety layer only, held-out set of 20)

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Harmful requests refused | 60% |
| Self-harm prompts answered with support | 100% |
| Harmless prompts wrongly refused | 0% |

Patterns were widened using gaps in the training seed set, not the evaluation set, so these numbers are not tuned to the test. The remaining harmful prompts are meant to be handled by safety training; model-level numbers will be published once a THOR chat model is trained.

## Consequences

- Fewer usable datasets, but every token has a known, permitted origin.
- Keyword screening has blind spots and can't understand intent; it's a floor, not a ceiling. A learned safety classifier is a future upgrade.
- Surrogate replacement changes some text; the curation report counts every replacement per source.
